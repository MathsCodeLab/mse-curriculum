# Lesson 1 - Numbers: The Foundation of Computing

## Objective

To understand what numbers are, why humans invented them, and why they are fundamental to mathematics, computer science, and programming.

---

## Reflection

### What is a number?

A number is an abstract mathematical concept used to represent quantity, position, or value. Digits are simply the symbols we use to write numbers. The same number can be represented using different numeral systems, such as decimal, binary, or Roman numerals.

---

### Why do humans need numbers?

Humans need numbers to count, measure, compare, communicate quantities, and keep track of information. Numbers are essential in everyday life, including financial transactions, dates and time, cooking, transportation, engineering, science, and technology.

---

### Why is place value important?

Place value determines the value of a digit, based on its position within a number. It is one of the foundations of our base-10 number system and is essential for arithmetic, algebra, and many mathematical concepts, such as factors and divisibility.

## What I learned

One of the most important concepts I revisisited was the divisibility rule for 9.

Instead of performing long division, I learned that I can simply add the digits of a number together. If the resulting sum is divisible by 9, then the original number is also divisible by 9.

For example:

Given 39,

3 + 9 = 12 

1 + 2 = 3

Since 3 is not divisible by 9, 39 is not divisible by 9.

I also discovered why this rule works. It comes from our base-10 number system.

For example:

39 = 30 + 9

= 3 x 10 + 9

Since 10 = 9 + 1,

39 = 3 x (9 + 1) + 9

= (3 x 9) + (3 x 1) + 9

= (3 x 9) + 3 + 9

(3 x 9) is a multiple of 9 and can be ignored, leaving (3 + 9) i.e. sum of the original digits. This helped me understand that mathematics is based on logical patterns and not memorized tricks or magic, lol.
